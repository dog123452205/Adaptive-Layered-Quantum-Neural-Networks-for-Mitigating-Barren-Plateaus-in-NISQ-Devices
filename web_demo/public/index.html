⚛ AL-QNN — Quantum vs Classical

What is quantum computing?

Instead of classical 0/1 bits, a quantum circuit uses qubits that can be in a superposition of states. Input data is encoded as rotation angles on each qubit (angle encoding); quantum gates (rotation, entangling) transform that state, and measuring the expectation value yields a classification probability.

Why quantum for this problem?

A quantum circuit can represent an exponentially large feature space with very few qubits (n qubits ↔ 2ⁿ dimensions), a potential path to far fewer trainable parameters than a classical network. AL-QNN also adapts its own circuit depth (adding/pruning layers) to avoid barren plateaus — vanishing gradients in circuits that grow too deep.

Confidence distribution — why is AL-QNN's confidence low?

Each bar is the share of test samples whose predicted P(positive) falls in that range. AL-QNN is kept shallow on purpose (2–4 layers) to avoid barren plateaus — vanishing gradients in deep circuits — so its output rarely strays far from 0.5. Logistic Regression has no such depth constraint and pushes probabilities toward 0 or 1.
